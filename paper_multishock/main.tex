\begin{abstract}
\noindent Who bore Britain's trade disruptions of 2021 to 2026, and
how much did the tax and benefit system give back? This paper studies
five events (the EU customs border, the 2022 energy price surge, US
tariffs, and the India and CPTPP agreements) in a policy-simulation
design: the size of each event is imported from published estimates
and traced to households, with the two largest run through the
statutory tax and benefit system on \SsHouseholdsRecords\ household
records representing \SsWeightedHouseholdsM\ million households in
PolicyEngine UK. Three results. The price rises from the border and
the energy surge fell hardest on lower-income households. The system
offered no automatic protection, and the annual uprating lagged
inflation by a year, costing benefit recipients roughly \pounds 12 to
15 billion; emergency support returned 50 to 77 per cent of the
energy loss, while the food price rise had no instrument aimed at it.
Around nine tenths of the variation in exposure lies within income
deciles.
\end{abstract}
