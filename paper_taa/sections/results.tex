\section{First-pass costings}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Eligible flows}

Over the four quarters to June 2026, ONS redundancy data give an annual
flow of \TaaFlowManuf\ manufacturing redundancies and \TaaFlowAll\ across
all industries; the trade-exposed memo population is \TaaFlowExposed. One
caveat travels with the narrow flow: the final quarter's manufacturing
figure (\TaaRedManufAprJunTwentySix) is anomalously low against the three
preceding quarters (\TaaRedManufJulSepTwentyFive,
\TaaRedManufOctDecTwentyFive, \TaaRedManufJanMarTwentySix)---LFS
redundancy cells are small and noisy---so the narrow costings likely sit
toward the low end of their steady-state range. A single-quarter audit
makes the flow sensitivity concrete: annualising each quarter alone spans
roughly 16{,}000 to 93{,}000 manufacturing redundancies a year, so every
narrow cost cell inherits a wide flow band before any scenario dial
moves. The costings below therefore lean on the
four-quarter sum, and Section~\ref{sec:results-cyclical} bounds the
cyclical dimension explicitly.

Per-beneficiary magnitudes make the arms comparable to existing
benefits. The central wage-insurance payment is
\pounds\TaaWageInsPerWorkerCentralManuf\ a year for a re-employed
manufacturing worker (half of a 21.5 per cent earnings loss on the
\pounds\TaaMeanWeeklyManuf\ mean full-time manufacturing wage)---roughly
the annual value of the proposed Unemployment Insurance itself. The UI
extension is worth \pounds\TaaUiExtPerWorkerCentral\ per claimant (the
gap between \pounds\TaaUiWeekly\ a week for nine months and the current
\pounds\TaaJsaWeekly\ New Style JSA for six). The retraining stipend is
\pounds\TaaTrainPerWorker\ flat; the UC bound prices six months of the
single-adult standard allowance for capital-gated claimants.

The historical design provides an external benchmark inside the surface.
ISERBS topped a re-employed steelworker's pay up to 90 per cent of prior
earnings for 78 weeks: at the central penalty that implies a top-up of
11.5 per cent of prior pay---almost identical per week to Arm A's 50 per
cent of a 21.5 per cent gap (10.75 per cent)---but over a shorter
horizon. Applied to today's narrow flow, the ISERBS parameterisation
costs \pounds\TaaIserbsCost\,million a year
(\pounds\TaaIserbsPerWorker\ per re-employed worker): the scheme the UK actually ran for three decades is,
in gross cost, \emph{cheaper} than the US-style arm costed here. A
British government seeking a precedent-anchored design could restore the
ISERBS parameters for all manufacturing redundancies for less than the
headline arm.

\subsection{Cyclical stress}\label{sec:results-cyclical}

Redundancy flows are strongly cyclical, and an insurance scheme priced on
recent flows must expect to pay out multiples of its steady-state cost
precisely when it is most needed. Scaling the central narrow programme by
historical manufacturing redundancy flows: at the 2009 financial-crisis
peak (\TaaFlowRatioGFC\ times the current flow) the programme costs \pounds\TaaCostPeakGFC\,million; at the
2020 pandemic peak, \pounds\TaaCostPeakCovid\,million; at the 1996--2025
historical median (share-scaled from the all-industry series, a labelled
assumption), \pounds\TaaCostHistMedian\,million. The linear scaling
ignores the recession-time deterioration in re-employment rates, which
would shift cost from the wage-insurance arm toward the UI arm; the
direction of the total is nonetheless clear, and any fiscal provision for
a statutory scheme should be set against the peak, not the recent flow.

\subsection{The self-financing threshold}\label{sec:results-breakeven}

The US evidence's central claim is behavioural: wage insurance shortens
non-employment enough to pay for itself \citep{hyman2024}. The UK
statutory arithmetic makes the threshold precise. One month less
non-employment for a central displaced manufacturing worker is worth
\pounds\TaaFiscalPerMonth\ to the Exchequer: the benefit payments not
made (Universal Credit standard allowance plus contributory JSA under
the first-six-months convention) plus the income tax and employee
National Insurance paid on a month of re-employment earnings at the
post-displacement wage. Against Arm~A's central per-worker cost of
\pounds\TaaWageInsPerWorkerCentralManuf\ a year, the arm is
self-financing if insured workers return to work
\TaaBreakEvenMonths\ months earlier on average than they otherwise
would---comfortably inside the several-months reduction implied by the
\citet{hyman2024} employment effects over a two-year horizon, though
that comparison imports US behaviour into UK institutions and is
illustrative, not predictive. The threshold is also a lower bound in one
mechanical respect (the benefit-saving convention adds UC and JSA where
a real claimant may hold only one) and an upper bound in another (it
credits no UC taper clawback of the wage-insurance payment itself, which
the microsimulation stage will net off). What the arithmetic
establishes is the order of magnitude: the behavioural response needed
for self-financing is months, not years, and well within the observed
range of the only causal estimates in existence.

\subsection{Who the scheme would cover}\label{sec:results-composition}

The eligible flow is not a cross-section of the workforce. Of
all-industry redundancies over the costing year, \TaaEligMaleShare\ per
cent are men and \TaaEligFiftyPlusShare\ per cent are aged 50 or
over---over a third of the flow sits in the age group for which the US
wage-insurance evidence is identified (the age-50 eligibility
discontinuity) and for which UK displacement losses are largest and
re-employment slowest \citep{hijzen2010}. The composition matters for
design: an instrument whose evidence base is strongest for older
workers is being costed here for a flow disproportionately made of
them. Industry-level demographic splits are not published at usable
cell sizes; the microsimulation stage will carry the full
household-composition incidence.

\subsection{Scale: the ad-hoc comparison}

Government money committed around the two current steel interventions is
roughly \pounds\TaaSteelSupportTotal\,million
(\pounds\TaaSteelPortTalbot\,million at Port Talbot,
\pounds\TaaSteelScunthorpe\,million at Scunthorpe to January 2026, the
latter still accruing). The central narrow-eligibility programme---all
four arms, every manufacturing redundancy in the country---costs
\TaaTotalCentralPctSteelNarrow\ per cent of that; the trade-exposed memo
programme costs \TaaTotalCentralPctSteelExposed\ per cent. A designed,
national, rules-based insurance system for manufacturing displacement is,
in gross annual cost, the same kind of money the UK has committed to two
plants in two years. The broad programme is different money
(\TaaTotalCentralPctSteelBroad\ per cent of the steel anchor): extending
trade-adjustment instruments into general redundancy insurance is a
welfare-state reform, not a trade policy, and the bracket between the
narrow and broad columns is the price of avoiding a certification
mechanism.

The comparison cuts both ways and should be read symmetrically: the
steel money buys plant continuity, supply-chain security and local
transition support, not only worker insurance; and the insurance
programme would cover workers the ad-hoc packages never reach. What the
juxtaposition establishes is narrower and robust to the caveats---the
fiscal scale of a statutory instrument is not what has prevented one.
