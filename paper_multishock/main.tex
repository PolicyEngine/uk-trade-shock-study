\begin{abstract}
\noindent Between 2021 and 2026 Britain experienced five major trade
events: the EU customs border, the 2022 energy price surge, US
tariffs, and the India and CPTPP agreements. This paper asks who bore
each of them and how much the tax--benefit system gave back---a policy
simulation, not causal estimation, with shock sizes imported from
published estimates and traced onto household records in PolicyEngine
UK. The harmful price shocks---dearer food after Brexit, dearer
energy---hit poorer households hardest. The benefit system offered no
automatic protection: British benefits respond to falling incomes,
not rising prices, and the annual uprating lagged a year behind,
worth roughly \pounds 12--15 billion in the crisis year. Emergency
support returned half to three-quarters of the energy shock, most
demand-efficiently through targeted payments; the Brexit food shock
had no instrument aimed at it. Around nine-tenths of the variation in
exposure lies within income deciles, so income-targeted support
struggles to reach the worst-affected.
\end{abstract}
