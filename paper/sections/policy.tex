\section{Policy}\label{sec:policy}

The deal-counterfactual and pharmaceutical-channel exercises that earlier
versions reported here have been withdrawn: both priced a change in the
declared sector shock through a calibration that its own rank-order check
does not corroborate, and the paired contrast was smaller than its own
assignment dispersion. The machinery remains in the repository.


\subsection{Cushioning policy}\label{sec:policy-reforms}

The simulations support a limited conclusion, and it is not the one an earlier draft drew. The headline gap is a tax-progressivity effect: income tax and employee National Insurance absorb \DispChannelTaxAndNI\ per cent of the displaced gross loss against \DispChannelBenefits\ per cent for all modelled benefits. Means-tested support is not what makes concentrated losses expensive; the marginal-versus-average structure of the tax schedule is. The take-up experiments sharpen where the policy question sits. The new-entitlement claiming rate is not the binding parameter---indeed it binds on nobody, since almost every displaced household is already UC-entitled in work---so the leverage lies entirely with the gates this model holds fixed or cannot exercise. Three matter, and they do not point the same way. Claiming among long-standing eligible non-claimants is worth about \TakeupEntitledSpread\ percentage points of displacement cushioning (on the source and calibration set out in Section~\ref{sec:results-takeup}), and the absence of New Style JSA a further \JSACushionPoints\ points: both mean the model \emph{understates} the protection a displaced worker would receive. The unmodelled capital test---redundancy payments against thresholds frozen since 2006---runs the other way and means it overstates protection. The net sign is therefore not established, and the policy question is which of these gates binds hardest in practice rather than how large the modelled cushion is. Automatic enrolment after redundancy, contribution-based unemployment insurance and wage insurance are therefore candidate counterfactuals for future analysis, not policy recommendations established by the present results. Their costs and incidence require dedicated simulations and financing assumptions.

The repository also supplies reusable interfaces for the next policy exercises.
Observable household and worker characteristics can tilt shock probabilities
while preserving each sector's aggregate earnings loss, and a separate
price-channel interface converts expenditure-weighted price changes into real
disposable income. Neither interface supplies causal coefficients: the former
requires externally estimated heterogeneity and the latter requires household
expenditure data. They are extensions to populate and validate, not additional
headline estimates in this paper.
