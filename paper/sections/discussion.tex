\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

The two negative price shocks of 2021--26, one policy-made and one
transmitted through the terms of trade, were regressive. The projected gains from the UK--India
agreement are small and similar shares of spending across deciles,
because they fall on clothing and footwear, whose budget shares are
flat across the distribution.

The state responded through different mechanisms. Where the shock hit
earnings, in the tariff scenario, the automatic tax--benefit system
absorbed part of the loss. Where it hit prices, cash benefits did not
respond within the year and indexation ran a year late
(Section~\ref{sec:results-indexation}); discretionary instruments did
the work instead. They cushioned the energy shock at rates that fall
with income above the third decile, energy-specific instruments
returning \SsEnergySpecificRatePct\ per cent of the counterfactual
cost and the full stack more than all of it in the bottom four deciles
at the paper's rebase, while the TCA food shock, with a similar spending-share
gradient, had no instrument legislated against it
(Section~\ref{sec:results-tca-ss}). The two episodes differed in size
and speed, and the paper does not estimate why the responses differed.

Most of the variance in the energy burden lies \emph{within} income
deciles, the pattern from which \citet{levell2025} conclude that an
income-conditioned instrument targets energy exposure poorly. On the
government's own projections the positive episode's gains are small
and flat; on published estimates the negative episodes are large and
regressive. The comparison is of sizes and shapes, not of the merits
of the policies.

The results carry several limitations. Three shocks are
taken from the literature with their own identification and the fourth is assumed; the incidence is first-order
and static; the price and earnings channels are not combined on common
households; and the decomposition labels all legislated change as
discretionary. The consumption imputation carries its own error. Deflating
2024--25 incomes to the rule year leaves the full-stack rate at
\SsDeflFullRatePct\ per cent and moves no decile by more than
\SsDeflMaxDecileDiffPp\ points, though weights are not rescaled
(Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}). Natural extensions are to combine the two
channels on the same households, to apply the rulebook axis
to every episode, and to replace the fixed-weight indices with the
non-homothetic cost-of-living indices of \citet{hochmuth2025}.
