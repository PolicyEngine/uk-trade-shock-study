\begin{abstract}
\noindent Britain's post-Covid decade delivered three trade-policy
shocks---the TCA border (2021), the US tariffs (2025), the India
agreement (2026)---plus the sharpest trade-transmitted terms-of-trade
shock since the 1970s (2022) and a near-zero benchmark (CPTPP). This
paper studies them under one household lens: each episode a declared
first stage from published estimates or government projections, mapped
onto households through prices and earnings, compared in ratios, with
the policy/market distinction explicit. First-pass results: the
negative shocks were regressive (on its realised path the energy shock
cost the bottom decile \MsEnergyRealisedPctSpendDecOne\ per cent of
spending versus \MsEnergyRealisedPctSpendDecTen\ at the top; TCA food
\MsTcaWindowAvgPctSpendDecOne\ versus \MsTcaWindowAvgPctSpendDecTen\
on the window-average path), the projected positive gains are
proportional and negligible, and the cushioning was margin-specific:
automatic on earnings
(\MsUSTariffDisplacementCushionPct--\MsUSTariffWageCutCushionPct\ per
cent), discretionary on prices (the Energy Price Guarantee absorbed
\MsEnergyCushionShareFyPct\ per cent of the counterfactual shock on the
paper's financial-year basis, and
Ofgem's component data put \MsEnergyWholesaleShareOfRisePct\ per cent
of the cap's rise in traded wholesale energy), with
indexation a year late---\pounds\MsUcLagCostFlatAltGbp\ to
\pounds\MsUcLagCostGbp\ for a single adult's Universal Credit
standard allowance in the crisis year, by convention. Running the largest episode through the statutory system on
\SsHouseholdsRecords\ enhanced-FRS households sharpens the picture for
that episode. The statutory system's automatic response to the price shock is
structurally zero: every means-tested award conditions on nominal
income, which a price shock does not touch---a property of the
rulebook, which the calculator's shocked re-run confirms rather than
measures. The system's one automatic price-side channel, indexation,
arrived late---the April 2022 uprating left benefit income
\SsUpratingShortfallPctOfBenefitIncome\ per cent short in real terms
over the year. What cushioned was discretionary and progressive: the energy-specific
instruments returned \SsGridPaperEbssRatePct\ per cent of the
counterfactual shock, and the full stack---including cost-of-living
payments legislated against the wider inflation episode, an
attribution choice stated in the text---\SsDiscretionaryRatePct\ per
cent, \SsCushionPctDecOne\ per cent of the bottom decile's loss
against \SsCushionPctDecTen\ per cent of the top's---correcting a
first-pass inference drawn from the price cap alone. And on the preferred basis
\SsWithinPct\ per cent of the variance in exposure lies within income
deciles rather than between them, so the regressive gradient is real
and is a small part of who was exposed.
\end{abstract}
