\begin{abstract}
\noindent The United Kingdom protects firms from trade shocks but has no
statutory instrument for the workers those shocks displace. Since 2024 the
government has committed roughly
\pounds\InstAdHocSteelTotal\,million of ad-hoc support around two steel
interventions, none of it designed or costed as worker-side insurance,
while the trade strategy expands firm-side defence instruments. This paper
designs four candidate trade-adjustment policies---wage insurance on the US
model, the government's own proposed Unemployment Insurance applied to
displaced workers, a retraining stipend, and a Universal Credit
redundancy-pay disregard---and produces first-pass annual costings from
public data under narrow (manufacturing) and broad (all-redundancy)
eligibility with bracketed take-up. Central estimates put the four arms together at
\pounds\TaaTotalCostCentralNarrow\,million a year under narrow
eligibility---\TaaTotalCentralPctSteelNarrow\ per cent of the ad-hoc
steel support the UK has committed since 2024. A companion microsimulation stage, specified here, will
locate the household-level incidence and net fiscal cost of each arm within
the tax--benefit system, where means-testing claws back gross payments.
\end{abstract}
