\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper examines the household incidence of five UK trade events
of 2021 to 2026 (the EU customs border, the 2022 energy price surge,
US tariffs, and the India and CPTPP agreements) and how far the tax
and benefit system cushioned each. The analysis is a policy
simulation: each event's magnitude is imported from published
estimates and traced to households, and the two largest episodes are
additionally run through the statutory system in PolicyEngine UK. The negative price events were
regressive, hitting lower-income deciles hardest. The system provided no automatic
offset to price rises; uprating lagged inflation by one year, a delay priced by a
rulebook-vintage run at roughly \pounds 15 billion; the full
discretionary stack returned 60 to 77 per cent of the counterfactual
energy shock across the sensitivity grid; the post-Brexit food price
rise attracted no dedicated instrument. On imputed spending data,
over ninety per cent of exposure variance arises within income
deciles.
\end{abstract}
