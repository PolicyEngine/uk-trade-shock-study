\section{Shock mechanics}\label{app:mechanics}

The primary estimator is the balanced repeated assignment of Section~\ref{sec:method-margins}, which conditions on the weighted wage-bill and headcount targets and therefore does \emph{not} preserve exact record-level marginal inclusion probabilities. The Bernoulli comparator, in which each employee in exposed division $j$ is independently selected with probability $s_j$, preserves those probabilities exactly and meets the targets only in expectation; it is reported alongside the balanced design throughout. Selected workers have employment income, hours, pension contributions and statutory pay zeroed. Wage cuts remove $s_j$ of each exposed worker's earnings deterministically. Reallocation uses the same selected workers, assigns a services destination, and applies the calibrated earnings penalty.

UC claiming is redrawn after every margin under the default \texttt{new\_entitlement} scope: benefit units whose circumstances changed \emph{and} whose all-claim award moves from zero at baseline to positive post-shock. As Section~\ref{sec:results-takeup} reports, that set is \emph{not} empty: \TakeupRedrawnBenunits\ benefit units are re-drawn per assignment on average, and the claiming rate moves displacement cushioning by \TakeupConventionSpread\ points. The \texttt{all\_entitled} scope, which redraws every changed entitled unit, has been run at the unit stress and is the source quoted in Section~\ref{sec:results-takeup}. The LCWRA override sets the benefit-unit element to the maximum of its stored baseline value and one statutory annual element, preventing duplicate payment.

\section{Monte Carlo and measures}\label{app:mc}

Central direct and measured scenarios use \ProductionDraws\ assignments. Every displayed $\pm$ value is an assignment SD, not a standard error or confidence interval. The machine-readable artifacts additionally store numerical Monte Carlo standard error as assignment SD divided by $\sqrt{n}$; it measures precision of the simulated mean only. Survey-design, tariff-response and model-parameter uncertainty are not estimated. Five-assignment mechanism, demographic, poverty-gap, reallocation, duration and sensitivity-grid exercises, and the ten-assignment supply-chain exercise, are exploratory and are excluded from the headline evidence. The take-up grid is scored over \TakeupSeeds\ assignments of the Bernoulli comparator design and reported in the main text; the leave-one-division-out audit uses \LeaveSectorDraws.

Reported relative BHC poverty changes hold the poverty line frozen at 60 per cent of the baseline equivalised median; absolute BHC poverty is a different measure: it uses PolicyEngine UK's \texttt{in\_absolute\_poverty\_bhc}, whose line is the fixed 2010--11 HBAI threshold, not the baseline median of this simulation. The two therefore do not coincide, and the absolute measure records a larger change than the relative one on the same runs. The cushioning rate is $1-\Delta Y/\Delta E$, where $\Delta E$ is gross employment-earnings loss and $\Delta Y$ is household disposable-income loss.

\paragraph{Reconciling cushioning and the Exchequer effect.} This reconciliation is computed on the \ProductionDraws-draw Bernoulli comparator, not on the \SubmissionDraws-draw balanced design of Table~\ref{tab:cushioning}; the two report different Exchequer levels for the same scenario and should not be read across. The Exchequer effect is the change in the aggregate simulated government balance, not the household-side transfer implied by the cushioning identity. The identity is $(1-\Delta Y/\Delta E)\times\Delta E=\Delta E-\Delta Y$, and on a stochastic margin it must be evaluated draw by draw and then averaged: the ratio and the gross loss are correlated across draws, so the product of their means is a different statistic. The household-side offset is therefore \pounds\FullWageImpliedOffset\ million for the full-schedule wage cut and \pounds\FullDisplacedImpliedOffset\ million for displacement, against reported Exchequer costs of \pounds\FullWageExchequer\ million and \pounds\FullDisplacedExchequer\ million. (The wage-cut margin is deterministic in its gross loss, so there the two constructions coincide and $\FullWageCushion$ per cent of \pounds\FullWageGross\ million does reproduce the offset; on the displacement margin it does not, and multiplying the two displayed figures together will not give \pounds\FullDisplacedImpliedOffset\ million.) The wedge is revenue lost outside employee disposable income: employer National Insurance contributions fall with the employee wage bill under every margin, and for displaced workers the zeroing of employee pension contributions and salary-sacrifice arrangements changes taxable pay relative to the gross-earnings accounting in $\Delta E$. The government balance also absorbs any other simulated fiscal flows that respond to the shock. The household-side offset therefore falls short of the Exchequer cost by \pounds\FullDisplacedExchequerWedge\ million on the displacement margin and \pounds\FullWageExchequerWedge\ million on the wage-cut margin, and the two statistics answer different questions: the cushioning rate measures household income protection per pound of employee earnings lost, while the Exchequer effect measures the total first-round cost to the public finances. Every figure in this paragraph is generated from the stored artifacts rather than multiplied out in the text.

\section{Duration scope}\label{app:durationtakeup}

The submission design reports three-, six- and twelve-month
\emph{duration-equivalent annual stresses}. It scales the probability of a
coherent full-year displaced state rather than combining partial-year earnings
with full-year unemployment status. These rows preserve expected worker-month
loss but are not unemployment-spell simulations. Actual within-year tax, UC,
waiting-period and re-employment timing requires a monthly model and is not
claimed here.

\section{Monthly versus annual UC accounting}\label{app:monthlyuc}

The annual model represents an $m$-month unemployment spell as a
probability $m/12$ of a coherent full-year displaced state. This appendix
bounds the resulting bias with statutory 2026 parameters for a
representative single displaced worker aged 25 or over, without children,
housing element or capital above the lower limit, at the exposed-sector FRS
mean wage of \pounds 48{,}272. UC entitlement per worker-month is identical
by construction: $m$ months at the full standard allowance
(\pounds\MonthlyUCStandardAllowance\ a month; earnings above the taper-out
point in the working months) equals probability $m/12$ of twelve months.
The differences run through income tax and timing, and the mechanism is the
same marginal-versus-average effect that drives the paper's main result. A
partial-year loss removes earnings from the top of the worker's schedule, so
it is relieved at the marginal rate. Zeroing a full year removes the untaxed
personal allowance as well, so the same earnings are relieved at the lower
\emph{average} rate; scaling that by the duration then understates the
relief a real spell would attract. At this wage---below the higher-rate
threshold, so basic rate and employee National Insurance throughout---the
correct partial-year PAYE relief for a three-month spell is
\pounds\MonthlyUCTaxMonthlyThree\ against
\pounds\MonthlyUCTaxAnnualThree\ in the annual equivalent, so the annual
model \emph{understates} total cushioning for a three- or six-month spell
by about \MonthlyUCCushionGap\ percentage points
(\MonthlyUCCushionMonthly\ versus \MonthlyUCCushionAnnual\ per cent) and is
exact at twelve months. The five-week UC payment wait shifts timing, not
annual entitlement. The primary 12-month contrast in
Table~\ref{tab:cushioning} is therefore unaffected, and the shorter
duration-equivalent rows are conservative for the displacement margin:
correcting the annualisation would raise displacement cushioning at those
scales and narrow, not widen, the reported gap. Parameters and the full
accounting are stored in \path{results/referee_fixes.json}.
