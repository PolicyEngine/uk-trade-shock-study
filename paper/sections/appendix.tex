\section{Data and conventions}\label{app:data}

\subsection{Household-stage conventions}\label{app:conventions}

\runin{Exposure variable.} Household energy exposure is the model's
domestic energy consumption, electricity plus gas, with a weighted
mean of \pounds 1{,}632 at 2024--25 prices. The mean divides out of
every aggregate through the rebase.

\runin{Instruments.} The Energy Price Guarantee enters as the
difference between the two price paths. The Energy Bills Support
Scheme enters as \pounds 400 to every household; the modelled
\pounds\SsEbssBn\ billion compares with an outturn of
\pounds\SsEbssOutturnBn\ billion \citep{nao2024}. The Council Tax
energy rebate enters as \pounds 150 to every household in bands A to D
in England, Scotland and Wales, \pounds\SsCtrBn\ billion to
\SsCtrHouseholdsM\ million households, and adds \SsCtrRateAddPp\
points to the energy-specific and full-stack rates; the modelled
\pounds\SsCtrEnglandBn\ billion for England compares with
\pounds\SsCtrOutturnEnglandBn\ billion paid by the end of September
2022 \citep{dluhcctr}. The Cost of Living Payments follow PolicyEngine
UK's 2022--23 eligibility rules (the means-tested payment per benefit
unit, the pensioner payment through Winter Fuel Payment eligibility
and \pounds 150 per disabled person), \pounds\SsColBn\ billion in
total; eligibility in the simulated year is not receipt in the 2022
qualifying windows. DWP management information \citep{dwpcol} puts the
three payments at \pounds\SsColOutturnTotalBn\ billion, so the model
is \SsColOutturnTotalExcessPct\ per cent high (percentages on
unrounded values):
\SsColOutturnMtExcessPct\ per cent on the means-tested payment
(\pounds\SsColModelMtBn\ against \pounds\SsColOutturnMtBn\ billion),
\SsColOutturnPenExcessPct\ on the pensioner payment
(\pounds\SsColModelPenBn\ against \pounds\SsColOutturnPenBn) and
\SsColOutturnDisExcessPct\ on the disability payment
(\pounds\SsColModelDisBn\ against \pounds\SsColOutturnDisBn). Scaling
each to its official figure moves the full-stack rate from
\SsDiscretionaryRatePct\ to \SsColOutturnCalFullRatePct\ per cent, with
the highest decile at \SsColOutturnCalFullMaxDecilePct. The Warm Home
Discount and discretionary council funds are not modelled, so the
total is an approximation with omissions on both sides, not a bound.

\runin{Weights and shares.} Person weights gross to \SsPersonsM\
million and household weights to \SsWeightedHouseholdsM\ million,
against an ONS count of \SsOnsHouseholdsM\ million households, so
aggregates sit about \SsWeightExcessPct\ per cent high; ratios are
unaffected. Deciles are of equivalised income on the Households Below
Average Income (HBAI) before-housing-costs concept. Burdens are shares
of household expenditure, because a ratio to income is dominated by
very low incomes. The numerator is rebased to 2021--22 cap levels and
the denominator to the ONS FYE~2022 mean total spending (factor
\SsSpendRebase), so household-stage shares are comparable in level
with the published-table shares. Both factors are common scalars, so
the variance decomposition and quantile ratios are invariant to them.

\runin{Deflated inputs.} The households carry 2024--25 nominal
incomes. Deflating \SsDeflInputsScaled\ monetary inputs to 2023--24 by
the model's uprating indices (earnings by
\SsDeflEarningsFactorTwentyThreePct\ per cent) gives a full-stack rate
of \SsDeflFullRatePct\ per cent (\SsDeflFullRateDiffPp\ points) and
moves no decile by more than \SsDeflMaxDecileDiffPp\ points; deflating
to 2022--23 (earnings by \SsDeflEarningsFactorTwentyTwoPct\ per cent)
under 2022--23 rules gives \SsDeflRulesTwentyTwoFullRatePct\ per cent.
Weights are not rescaled.

\runin{Synthetic records.} \SsNoSynRecordsExcluded\ records,
\SsNoSynWeightSharePct\ per cent of household weight, are synthetic
records built from the Survey of Personal Incomes. Excluding them and
rescaling the remaining \SsNoSynRecordsKept\ records to the same
totals gives a full-stack rate of \SsNoSynDiscretionaryRatePct\ per
cent (\SsNoSynFullRateDiffPp\ points), an energy-specific rate of
\SsNoSynEnergySpecificRatePct\ per cent and a within-decile variance
share of \SsNoSynWithinPct\ per cent; no decile rate moves by more
than \SsNoSynMaxDecileDiffPp\ points, and
\numword{\SsNoSynDecilesAboveHundred} deciles have point estimates above 100
per cent.

\subsection{The model's built-in support variables and the decile ranking}\label{app:native}

PolicyEngine UK contains its own Energy Price Guarantee variable,
which reads energy consumption: it pays \pounds\SsNativeEpgBn\ billion
in the 2023 baseline and rises by \pounds\SsNativeEpgDeltaBn\ billion
under the shocked inputs. That confirms the variable responds, but the
change is measured around the dataset's unrebased energy levels and is
not comparable to the \pounds\SsEpgBn\ billion computed here. The
paper classifies the Guarantee as discretionary and models it on the
price paths of Section~\ref{sec:secondstage-method}. The model's
Guarantee, its cost-of-living payment variable
(\pounds\SsNativeColBn\ billion at 2023--24 amounts) and its
\pounds\SsNativeEbrBn\ billion energy-bills rebate sit inside the
income that ranks the deciles. Re-ranking on income net of the three
moves the bottom decile's full-stack rate from \SsRankBaseDOne\ to
\SsRankNetDOne\ per cent and leaves the top decile's at
\SsRankNetDTen, so the ranking does not drive the gradient.

\subsection{Reconciliations}\label{app:recon}

\runin{Energy budget shares.} \citet{rf2022} reports energy budget shares of roughly 11 per cent in the second
decile against 6 per cent in the ninth; the shares computed here are
\MsEnergyBudgetSharePctDecTwo\ and \MsEnergyBudgetSharePctDecNine\ per
cent. The gap is definitional: this paper measures electricity and gas
only, as a share of total spending, on the FYE~2022 workbook, whereas
the comparator includes other fuels, is expressed against income and
is not a 2022--23 figure.

\runin{Household-stage and published-table aggregates.} On the same
price path the published tables put the counterfactual shock at
\pounds\MsEnergyGrossAggBn\ billion and the realised path at
\pounds\MsEnergyRealisedAggBn\ billion, against
\pounds\SsCounterAggBn\ and \pounds\SsRealisedAggBn\ billion on
household records. Two offsets separate them: the household weights,
\SsWeightExcessPct\ per cent above the ONS count, and the rebase,
which sets the mean bill to the 2021--22 cap mean rather than to the
ONS FYE~2022 mean spend. Ratios are unaffected.

\section{Energy robustness}\label{app:energy}

\subsection{The wholesale share of the energy price rise}\label{app:wholesale}

The retail cap bundles wholesale energy, which is traded, with
network, policy and operating costs, margin and VAT, which are
domestic. On Ofgem's component breakdown \citep{ofgem2021, ofgem2022}, wholesale
rose from \pounds\MsEnergyWholesaleBase\ to
\pounds\MsEnergyWholesalePeak\ of the typical bill, from
\MsEnergyWholesaleShareCapBasePct\ to
\MsEnergyWholesaleShareCapPeakPct\ per cent of the cap, so
\MsEnergyWholesaleShareOfRisePct\ per cent of the cap's rise is traded
energy. The world-market-linked part of the realised burden is
therefore about \pounds\MsEnergyRealisedTradeTransmittedAggBn\ billion
(\pounds\MsEnergyTradeTransmittedAggBn\ billion of the counterfactual
shock). Wholesale is not the same as imported: UK-produced gas
repriced too, and that transfer runs to British producers and, through
the Energy Profits Levy, the Exchequer. The share is computed from
the winter 2021--22 cap to the October 2022 cap, and applying it to
the realised burden assumes the Guarantee absorbed wholesale and other
costs in proportion. \citet{dhingrapage2023} find more than
\MsEnergyImportedShareLowerBound\ per cent of the CPI rise was
imported, a statistic about the aggregate index and an
order-of-magnitude check only.

\subsection{The rebase factor and the price path}\label{sec:results-grid}

Table~\ref{tab:grid} and Figure~\ref{fig:grid} vary the rebase factor
(\SsGridLowRebase, the paper's \SsRebaseFactor, 0.800 and 1.000, no
rebase) and build the stack up one instrument at a time. The
Guarantee's rate, \SsGridPaperEpgRatePct\ per cent, is invariant to
the rebase, because a price cap scales with the bill, and across
deciles. The full stack returns \SsGridPaperFullRatePct\ per cent of
the counterfactual shock at the paper's rebase and
\SsGridUnityFullRatePct\ per cent with none. In every cell that
includes a flat-cash instrument the rate peaks in the third decile and
is lower at the top than at the bottom, but levels move: on
the full stack \numword{\SsGridLowDecilesAboveHundred} deciles are returned
more than their cost at a factor of \SsGridLowRebase\ (aggregate rate
\SsGridLowFullRatePct\ per cent) and none at one (the highest is
\SsGridUnityFullMaxDecilePct). At the paper's rebase, on the headline
path and the full sample, only decile \SsDecilesAboveHundredList\
does (\SsGridPaperFullMaxDecilePct\ per cent), and only as a point
estimate; on the equal-half-year paths the bottom decile also exceeds
100 (Table~\ref{tab:paths}), and without the synthetic records
\numword{\SsNoSynDecilesAboveHundred} deciles do. The
highest decile rate reaches 100 per cent at a factor of
\SsGridMaxDecileRebaseAtHundred, or
\SsGridNoCtrMaxDecileRebaseAtHundred\ without the Council Tax energy
rebate. On the energy-specific stack no decile exceeds 100 per cent in
any cell. One external figure bears on the choice of cell: the
Guarantee's \pounds\SsEpgBn\ billion at the paper's rebase compares
with an outturn of \pounds 24.0 billion \citep{nao2024}, and with no
rebase it would be about a third higher.

The price path is varied separately, across the four conventions of
Table~\ref{tab:paths}. The
headline weights each quarter's cap by the model's consumption profile
(\SsSeasShareAprSepPct\ per cent in April to September,
\SsSeasShareOctDecPct\ in October to December, \SsSeasShareJanMarPct\
in January to March) and uses the caps Ofgem set, including
\pounds 4{,}279 for January to March 2023 \citep{ofgemcap2023}: a
counterfactual mean of \pounds\SsSeasCwActualCounterCap, a shock of
\pounds\SsSeasCwActualCounterAggBn\ billion, and rates of
\SsSeasCwActualEpgRatePct\ (Guarantee),
\SsSeasCwActualEbssRatePct\ (energy-specific) and
\SsSeasCwActualFullRatePct\ per cent (full stack). Holding the October
cap through March with the same weights gives
\pounds\SsSeasCwHeldCounterCap, \pounds\SsSeasCwHeldCounterAggBn\
billion and \SsSeasCwHeldEpgRatePct, \SsSeasCwHeldEbssRatePct\ and
\SsSeasCwHeldFullRatePct\ per cent. Weighting the half-years equally
gives \SsSeasEqActualEpgRatePct, \SsSeasEqActualEbssRatePct\ and

\SsSeasEqHeldEpgRatePct, \SsSeasEqHeldEbssRatePct\ and

\section{Indexation: Universal Credit arithmetic for a single adult}\label{sec:results-rulebook}

Universal Credit's standard allowance rose 3.1 per cent in April 2022,
indexed to the previous September's inflation, while inflation rose into double digits. Against month-by-month indexation to contemporaneous
CPI, an upper-bound counterfactual under which no actual benefit
system operates, a single adult's standard allowance fell
\pounds\MsUcLagCostGbp\ short over 2022--23
(\MsUcLagCostPctAllowance\ per cent of the annual allowance); against
a single April 2022 uprating at the April 2022 CPI inflation rate of
9.0 per cent in place of 3.1, \pounds\MsUcLagCostFlatAltGbp. Both figures are for the standard
allowance only, before elements and caseload weighting, and the range
is the result. Figure~\ref{fig:uprating-lag} shows the gap month by
month.

Neither the lag nor its cost is a discovery of this paper
\citep{cribb2023, obrwelfare2022}. The 2022--23 shortfall was a timing
loss later reversed in the level of awards, though not repaid;
below-inflation uprating in other years reflected freezes and caps
\citep{judgemurphy2023}. The 2022--23 shortfall followed the removal of the \pounds\MsUcUpliftRemovalGbp-a-year Covid uplift in October 2021,
months before the energy shock.

\section{The tariff earnings scenario}\label{app:e3}

\runin{Construction.} The shock to division $j$ is
$s_j=\tau_j x_j \varepsilon \pi$: the tariff rate $\tau_j$, the
US-export share of turnover $x_j$ (HMRC trade by industry over ONS
Annual Business Survey turnover, for \EThreeIndDivisions\ SIC
divisions; \citealp{hmrctrade, onsabs}), an export-demand elasticity
$\varepsilon$ and the wage-bill incidence $\pi$, both set to one as a
benchmark. The scenario omits price changes, imported inputs, profits
and general-equilibrium effects, and the sector weights are an
accounting bridge, not measured tariff incidence. Two margins deliver
the same gross loss, \pounds\EThreeDispGrossM\ million. Under the wage
cut every employee in division $j$ loses the share $s_j$ of earnings,
with pension contributions scaled. Under displacement each employee
loses the job for the full year with probability $s_j$,
independently: status becomes unemployed, and earnings, hours, pension
contributions and statutory pay are set to zero.

\runin{Simulation.} Baseline and shocked systems are run under
2026--27 rules on the Microcosm UK records, projected from 2024--25 to
2026--27 with the model's uprating indices (incomes and weights). The
cushioning rate is one minus the fall in HBAI household net income
over the fall in gross employee earnings. The displacement rate is the
exact expectation over the independent job losses, computed from all
\EThreeDispExposedRecords\ exposed records in
\EThreeDispExposedHouseholds\ households (about
\EThreeDispExactEffRecords\ effective; the largest record carries
\EThreeDispExactMaxShare\ per cent of the loss). It has no Monte Carlo
error; its standard error of \EThreeDispSamplingSe\ points is survey
sampling error, clustered on households. Benefit units newly entitled
to Universal Credit claim with an assumed probability of 0.8, mixed
exactly in the expectation; others keep their baseline flags, and
baseline poverty lines are frozen. The wage-cut rate mixes the same
two take-up passes exactly; only \numword{\EThreeWageRedrawnUnits} benefit
units are re-drawn. Displaced workers receive an approximation of
contribution-based New Style Jobseeker's Allowance:
\EThreeDispJsaWeeks\ weeks at the model's rates for those aged 18 to
state pension age with baseline earnings of at least 50 times the
weekly lower earnings limit, with no partner test and full take-up, an
upper bound since waiting days, pension abatement and sanctions are
omitted. It pays \pounds\EThreeDispJsaPaidM\ million, lowers Universal
Credit by \pounds\EThreeDispJsaUcOffsetM\ million and adds
\EThreeDispJsaEffectPts\ points to the rate. Redundancy pay,
re-employment and behavioural responses are not modelled, and reported
student-loan repayments are held fixed.

\runin{Uniform benchmark.} Spreading the same loss over all employees
in proportion to earnings gives cushioning rates of
\EThreeUnifWageRatePct\ per cent (wage cut) and
\EThreeUnifDispRatePct\ per cent (displacement), against the tariff
pattern's \EThreeWageRatePct\ and \EThreeDispRatePct\ (differences of \EThreeUnifWageGapPts\ and \EThreeUnifDispGapPts\
points; Table~\ref{tab:e3-split}). The largest component difference is
income tax under the wage cut (\EThreeUnifWageTaxGapPts\ points).

\runin{Exposed earnings against ASHE.} Weighted by expected
displacement, the exposed employees' mean earnings are
\pounds\EThreeAsheModelMeanK\ thousand against
\pounds\EThreeAsheMeanK\ thousand in the Annual Survey of Hours and
Earnings \citep[\EThreeAsheEdition\ edition, uprated
\EThreeAsheUpratingPct\ per cent to 2026--27 by the model's earnings
growth;][]{ashe} for \EThreeAsheDivisions\ of the \EThreeIndDivisions\
divisions (ASHE suppresses the estimate for tobacco), and the
mean of division medians is \pounds\EThreeAsheModelMedianK\ thousand
against \pounds\EThreeAsheMedianK\ thousand. The share above the
\pounds\EThreeHigherRateThreshold\ higher-rate threshold is
\EThreeHigherRateSharePct\ per cent in the model against about
\EThreeAsheHigherRateSharePct\ per cent implied by ASHE
(\EThreeAsheHigherRateLowPct--\EThreeAsheHigherRateHighPct). Modelled
exposed earnings are therefore, if anything, below ASHE, so the
income-tax relief is not inflated by overstated pay; the
division-level fit is noisy, and \EThreeHigherRateLossSharePct\ per
cent of the gross loss falls on employees above the threshold.

\runin{Sensitivity.} Universal Credit take-up of \EThreeSensTakeupLow\
and \EThreeSensTakeupHigh\ in place of 0.8 gives displacement rates of
\EThreeSensTakeupLowRatePct\ and \EThreeSensTakeupHighRatePct\ per
cent; 13 weeks of the New Style allowance gives
\EThreeSensJsaThirteenRatePct\ and none \EThreeSensJsaZeroRatePct.
Part-year spells are cushioned more: two approximations give
\EThreeSensSixMonthAnnualRatePct\ and \EThreeSensSixMonthMixRatePct\
per cent for six months and \EThreeSensThreeMonthAnnualRatePct\ and
\EThreeSensThreeMonthMixRatePct\ for three, because income tax then falls at marginal rates and the fixed weeks of allowance cover more
of the loss. At the OBR's elasticity of 0.4 the gross loss is
\pounds\EThreeObrLowWageGrossM\ million and the wage-cut rate
\EThreeObrLowWageRatePct\ per cent; the displacement rate is unchanged
by construction. A Monte Carlo check with \EThreeBernDraws\ Bernoulli
assignment draws and no New Style allowance gives a pooled rate of
\EThreeBernRatePooled\ per cent (standard error
\EThreeBernRatePooledSe), close to the exact rate without the
allowance (\EThreeDispNoJsaRatePct\ per cent). The Exchequer cost is
\pounds\EThreeWageExchequerM\ million (wage cut) and
\pounds\EThreeDispExchequerM\ million (displacement), above the
household-side offset because the government balance includes employer
contributions, and poverty before housing costs rises
\EThreeDispPovertyPp\ points under displacement.

\section{From incidence to demand and welfare}\label{sec:results-mpc}

This appendix aggregates the energy incidence into first-round demand
and welfare quantities. Every behavioural parameter is taken from the
literature and stated, nothing is estimated, and there is no
general-equilibrium or intertemporal response.

\runin{Demand.} Each flow of the energy episode, the counterfactual
burden and the four instruments, is weighted by a marginal propensity
to consume (MPC) assigned by income decile, under three assumed
scenarios: a gradient falling from 0.8 in the bottom decile to 0.3 in
the top, a deliberately steep stylisation of the gradients of
\citet{jappelli2014} and \citet{fagereng2021}; a flat 0.5; and an
asymmetric variant after \citet{bunn2018}, who report a mean MPC of
0.64 out of negative income shocks and 0.14 out of positive ones, here
0.5 for losses, below their estimate, and 0.14 for gains.
Table~\ref{tab:mpc} reports the results. Under the gradient the
counterfactual shock corresponds to a first-round consumption fall of
\pounds\SsMpcBurdenGradientBn\ billion (\pounds\SsMpcBurdenFlatBn\
billion with the flat MPC), and with the full stack the net
first-round effect is $\SsMpcNetGradientBn$ billion pounds. Under the
gradient only, first-round consumption per pound of outlay is
\SsMpcColPerPound\ for the Cost of Living Payments,
\SsMpcCtrPerPound\ for the Council Tax energy rebate,
\SsMpcEbssPerPound\ for the Energy Bills Support Scheme and
\SsMpcEpgPerPound\ for the Guarantee; the ordering is a property of
the assumed gradient. The asymmetric scenario applies the gain MPC to
every instrument, although the Guarantee truncates a loss rather than
paying a windfall; taken at face value the net effect is
$\SsMpcNetAsymBn$ billion pounds.

\runin{Welfare.} Weighting the counterfactual burden by
\citet{atkinson1970} welfare weights proportional to $y^{-\gamma}$ and
normalised to mean one, the simplest case of the weights of
\citet{saezstantcheva2016}, with equivalised income $y$ floored at
\pounds 1{,}000 a year, gives a ratio of the welfare-weighted burden
to that of an equal-pounds shock of the same total of
\SsWelfPremiumGammaOne\ at $\gamma=1$ and \SsWelfPremiumGammaTwo\ at
$\gamma=2$. Both are below one: the shock is regressive in shares but
larger in pounds at the top.
