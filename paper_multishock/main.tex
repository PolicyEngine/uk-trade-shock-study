\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper examines the household incidence of five UK trade events
of 2021 to 2026 (the EU customs border, the 2022 energy price surge,
US tariffs, and the India and CPTPP agreements) and how far the tax and
benefit system cushioned them, a policy simulation with each event's
magnitude imported from published estimates and the two largest
episodes run through the statutory system in PolicyEngine UK. The negative price events
were regressive, hitting lower-income deciles hardest. The system provided no automatic offset
to price rises; uprating lagged inflation by one year, a delay a
rulebook run prices at roughly \pounds 15 billion; the energy-specific
instruments returned roughly 50 to 60 per cent of the counterfactual
shock (60 to 77 counting general cost-of-living support); the
post-Brexit food price rise attracted no dedicated instrument. On imputed spending data, an
upper bound puts over ninety per cent of exposure variance within
income deciles.
\end{abstract}
