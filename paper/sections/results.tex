\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

All central results use FRS 2024--25, period 2026, and \ProductionDraws\ assignments. Values after $\pm$ are standard deviations across artificial assignments, not standard errors or confidence intervals. Numerical Monte Carlo SE (SD divided by $\sqrt{\ProductionDraws}$) is stored separately in each JSON artifact. Five-assignment diagnostics are labelled exploratory.

\subsection{Derived sector shocks}\label{sec:results-shocks}

The deterministic full-schedule direct earnings stress is \pounds\FullWageGross\ million annually; the EPD stress is \pounds\EPDWageGross\ million. These are scenario inputs generated by Equation~\eqref{eq:shock}, not estimated causal effects. The sector rates are exactly half those of the former $\varepsilon=2$ high case.

Table~\ref{tab:cushioning} reports the central worker, gross-loss, Exchequer and cushioning outcomes for both schedules and all three central margins. The full-schedule displacement and wage-cut cushioning rates differ in their means, while displacement retains material assignment dispersion. For the displacement mean, the numerical Monte Carlo SE is \FullDisplacedCushionMCSE\ percentage points for cushioning and \pounds\FullDisplacedExchequerMCSE\ million for Exchequer cost. These quantities measure simulation precision only. The decomposition remains economically informative: proportional cuts remove the top slice of earnings and therefore receive relief near marginal tax rates; displacement removes the whole earnings stream and receives relief nearer average rates, with UC providing part of the remainder.

Part of the wage-cut margin's higher measured cushioning reflects a pension-contribution channel rather than the tax--benefit system alone: employee pension and salary-sacrifice contributions are scaled proportionally with the earnings cut, so part of the gross earnings loss is absorbed by reduced retirement saving rather than by current disposable income, while the associated tax relief falls. This is a measurement choice---the HBAI disposable-income concept treats employee pension contributions as an outflow---and it carries a caveat: the implied loss of deferred consumption in retirement is not captured by the cushioning rate.

\paragraph{Rent-sharing-calibrated scenario.} The empirically calibrated mixed scenario of Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (survivor-wage share 0.15, displacement share 0.85) yields a cushioning rate of $\FullRentSharingCushion\pm\FullRentSharingCushionSD$ per cent and an Exchequer cost of \pounds $\FullRentSharingExchequer\pm\FullRentSharingExchequerSD$ million under the full schedule, and $\EPDRentSharingCushion\pm\EPDRentSharingCushionSD$ per cent and \pounds $\EPDRentSharingExchequer\pm\EPDRentSharingExchequerSD$ million under the EPD schedule. Both sit between the polar margins but much closer to the displacement pole, as the 85/15 split implies: under empirically calibrated pass-through, the tax--benefit system's cushioning of a trade-related earnings loss is far nearer the displacement case than the wage-cut upper bound.

\paragraph{Take-up sensitivity.} The regenerated grid applies the same post-shock-entitlement claiming rule to all margins. It uses only five assignments per cell and is exploratory. It shows that displacement responds more visibly to the assumed claiming probability, while wage-cut results move little because relatively few wage-cut households change UC entitlement. No grid difference is treated as statistically identified.

\subsection{Mechanism}\label{sec:results-mechanism}

The regenerated decomposition and gate audit are also five-assignment diagnostics. Income-tax relief is larger on the wage-cut margin than on displacement; UC is more important after displacement. The plain FRS input contains no usable household-capital measure, so the model cannot establish the empirical importance of UC's capital test. New-style JSA is not activated by the imposed transition. These omissions make the gate audit a description of the implemented model, not the full UK out-of-work system.

\subsection{Distributional incidence}\label{sec:results-margins}

Under full displacement, relative BHC poverty rises by $\FullDisplacedPoverty\pm\FullDisplacedPovertySD$ percentage points. Under the wage-cut scenario, relative BHC poverty is unchanged at displayed precision. The national poverty changes are economically small relative to the uncertainty omitted from the exercise and are not a headline estimate. They can coexist with large losses for selected households because the exposed population is limited and often above the poverty line before the shock. Figure~\ref{fig:decile-margins} shows the decile incidence of each margin: mean per-capita losses rise almost monotonically with baseline income and are largest in the top decile, where exposed-sector earnings are concentrated, with assignment dispersion widest at the top under the displacement-based margins.

\subsection{Fiscal incidence}\label{sec:results-fiscal}

Annual Exchequer costs are reported in the Exchequer column of Table~\ref{tab:cushioning}; Figure~\ref{fig:exchequer-draws} shows their distribution across assignments. The Exchequer effect is the change in the aggregate government balance---all modelled taxes minus all modelled benefit spending---and is therefore broader than the household cushioning identity: multiplying the cushioning rate by the gross employee earnings loss gives about \pounds 389 million for the full-schedule wage cut ($0.439\times\pounds 886$m) and about \pounds 325 million for displacement ($0.368\times\pounds 882$m), against reported Exchequer costs of \pounds\FullWageExchequer\ million and \pounds\FullDisplacedExchequer\ million. The \pounds 87--95 million wedge consists of revenue items that respond to the shock but sit outside employee disposable income---chiefly employer National Insurance contributions on the lost wage bill, plus the tax consequences of zeroed pension contributions and salary sacrifice for displaced workers. Appendix~\ref{app:mc} states the reconciliation formally.
