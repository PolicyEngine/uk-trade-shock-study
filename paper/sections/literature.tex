\section{Literature}\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Trade and households}

The method for carrying trade shocks to household microdata is the
first-order incidence approach of \citet{deaton1989} and
\citet{porto2006}, applied at scale by the World Bank's Household
Impacts of Tariffs programme and the OECD's concordance work
\citep{artucporto2021, artucporto2019, luu2020}.
\citet{fajgelbaumkhandelwal2016} show that the gains from trade flow
disproportionately to lower-income households through traded-goods
budget shares, and \citet{cravinolevchenko2017} that a large
devaluation raised lower-income households' cost of living by more.
\citet{jaravel2021} reviews evidence that trade affects price levels
similarly across income groups; the episodes here are consistent with
that in general, and regressive because they fell on food and energy
(Figure~\ref{fig:budget-shares}). \citet{borusyakjaravel2024} find
that most of the variance in welfare changes from trade shocks lies
within income deciles.

Evidence from the 2018--19 US tariffs finds near-complete pass-through
to US import prices
\citep{amitireddingweinstein2019, fajgelbaumetal2020, cavalloetal2021},
so a US tariff reaches the UK through export demand and earnings. The
2025 tariffs have been scored distributionally for US households
\citep{budgetlab2025, tpc2025, fleckpradhan2026} and modelled with
flexible and rigid wages \citep{ignatenko2025, rodriguezclare2025};
export-demand shocks pass into incumbent wages at elasticities well
below one \citep{hummels2014, garinsilverio2024}, which motivates
treating wage cuts and displacement as two margins. Work linking trade
models to microdata rarely carries statutory tax rules
\citep{robilliardrobinson2005, peichl2008, colombo2010, cali2019}. To
my knowledge no UK shock arriving under a trade label has been run
through a statutory tax--benefit calculator.

\subsection{Shocks through tax--benefit systems}

\citet{dolls2012} measure the income-stabilisation coefficients of the
US and European systems, \citet{brewertasseva2021} decompose the UK's
Covid response into automatic and discretionary parts, and
\citet{navicke2014} supply the labour-transition method.
\citet{cohenfollette2000} find in a macro model that automatic
stabilisers do very little against a supply shock, and only modestly
damp demand shocks. Two studies are closest to this one.
\citet{amores2025} run the 2022 inflation shock and its fiscal
measures through EUROMOD for the EU, separating income support from
price measures. \citet{popova2026} use UKMOD with imputed consumption
and an indexation counterfactual over 2019--23. Neither isolates the
September-CPI timing lag or the Energy Price Guarantee as a separate
layer, or treats a trade shock as the input. Related microsimulations
of the inflation episode include \citet{eckerstorfer2025},
\citet{sologon2025}, \citet{curci2025} and \citet{leventi2024}.

On indexation, \citet{paulus2020} treat non-indexation as a
distributional phenomenon. \citet{cribb2023} quantified the 2022--23
uprating shortfall (benefits rose 3.1 per cent against roughly ten per
cent inflation; recipients would not be made whole until April 2025 on
then-current forecasts) and scored the Cost of Living Payments against
timely indexation; \citet{obrwelfare2022} puts the real fall at about
\pounds 12 billion; and \citet{judgemurphy2023} show working-age
benefits were uprated below outturn inflation in ten of fifteen years.
This paper adds neither the discovery of the lag nor a better estimate
of it, only its incidence by decile beside the support that was paid.

\subsection{The UK episodes}

On energy, \citet{levell2025} find on transaction data that income
explains little of the variation in energy spending, and that an
optimal package retains a price subsidy even when transfers can
condition on income and past usage; \citet{chenlevell2024} show that
inflation inequality in 2021--23 also had a within-category margin
that expenditure tables miss. Most European crisis support was
untargeted \citep{ari2022, amaglobeli2026, bonnet2025, auclert2023},
and \citet{nao2024} records an estimated final cost of \pounds 44
billion for the UK's energy schemes, of which the Guarantee's outturn
was \pounds 24.0 billion. For the euro area \citet{pallotti2023} find
losses from the inflation shock roughly flat across incomes.

On Brexit, \citet{breinlich2016} and \citet{clarke2017} distributed
projected price changes across households before the border existed,
\citet{breinlich2022} find the depreciation's consumer cost roughly
flat across the income distribution, and \citet{bakker2023, bakker2026}
find the non-tariff-barrier effect on food prices regressive: the two
shocks fell on different baskets. Regional and labour-market studies
cover the earnings side
\citep{davenportlevell2021, alabrese2026, dhingra2026, freeman2022}.
None of these models the tax--benefit response, and the UK--India
impact assessment leaves household analysis to be ``developed further
for future FTA IAs'' \citep{rpc2025, dbtindia2026}.
