\begin{abstract}
\noindent Britain's post-Covid decade delivered four major trade
shocks and a placebo: the TCA border (2021), the sharpest
terms-of-trade deterioration since the 1970s (2022), the US tariffs
(2025), the India agreement (2026), and CPTPP accession. This paper
studies them under one household lens---each episode a declared first
stage from published estimates or government projections, mapped onto
households through prices and earnings, compared in ratios because
sizes span two orders of magnitude. First-pass results: the negative
shocks were regressive (energy cost the bottom decile
\MsEnergyGrossPctSpendDecOne\ per cent of spending versus
\MsEnergyGrossPctSpendDecTen\ at the top; TCA food
\MsTcaPctSpendDecOne\ versus \MsTcaPctSpendDecTen), the projected
positive gains are proportional and negligible, and the cushioning was
margin-specific: automatic on earnings
(\MsUSTariffDisplacementCushionPct--\MsUSTariffWageCutCushionPct\ per
cent), discretionary on prices (the Energy Price Guarantee absorbed
\MsEnergyCushionSharePct\ per cent), with indexation a year late---%
\pounds\MsUcLagCostGbp\ per Universal Credit household in the crisis
year. A verified PolicyEngine microsimulation stage is specified.
\end{abstract}
